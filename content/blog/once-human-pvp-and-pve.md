Once Human PvP and PvE punish the same overlay in different ways. A farm config that paints every container will bury a player peek. A tight PvP config will make you walk past loot you actually wanted.

Complete ships aimbot, player ESP, creature ESP, item ESP, radar, and misc on one key. Split them with saved profiles. Do not run max everything in both modes.

## PvP fights

Assume someone is recording. Tight FOV, hold hotkey, visibility check, chest bone, short ESP distance, corner boxes, names off. Creature radar dim or off. Item ESP limited to keys and high-value containers. Panic key bound where you can hit it.

Settlements and choke points are where snaplines and skeletons fight each other. Pick one. Cap distance. If FPS drops, skeleton goes first. See [ESP and radar](/blog/once-human-esp-and-radar/).

## PvE hunts and farming

Creature tracking and creature radar earn their place. Longer radar range helps in open terrain. Item and object ESP for resources, medical loot, and containers save time. Aimbot can stay modest; you are not trying to look like a clip against AI.

Keep player ESP on. Other Metas still show up on PvE routes. A farm session is not a reason to ignore PvP tags.

## Switching without rebuilding

Save `pvp-tight` and `pve-farm`. Load the PvP file before you walk into a contested compound. Load the farm file when you leave. Cloud save/load is listed in misc. Details in [config presets](/blog/once-human-hacks-config-presets/).

After a patch, test the PvP file first in a quiet area. If creature names shifted in object ESP, confirm checkout. We do not invent extra world POIs.

Risk does not change because you were "only farming." Read [account risk](/blog/once-human-hacks-account-risk/) before you treat PvE as harmless.
