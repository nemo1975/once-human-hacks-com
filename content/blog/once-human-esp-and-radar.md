Once Human ESP is the module group most buyers actually live with. Aimbot gets the screenshots. Overlays decide whether a settlement fight is readable or a mess of boxes.

This page covers player ESP, creature ESP, item ESP, object ESP, and the 2D radar as listed on Complete. Confirm the live chips on checkout.

## Player ESP first

Player and NPC ESP share the usual set: enemy and team flags, nickname, account ID, distance, health, shield, held item, kit, 2D boxes, corner boxes, skeleton, head indicators, direction indicators, snaplines, and a max distance cap.

In dense settlements, names plus snaplines plus skeleton is too much. Corner boxes with distance and health are enough for most PvP. Skeleton helps in interiors. Snaplines help orientation when you lose a target behind a building. Cap max distance so tags from the other side of the map do not eat FPS.

## Creature ESP is not a second player layer

Creature tracking, NPC creature markers, creature loot visualization, and creature radar are their own group. They exist so a large threat is not a surprise, not so you paint every animal on the server.

Leave creature radar on when you are farming. Turn opacity down when you are in a tight PvP compound. If a shop only lists "wallhack" with no creature group, you are not looking at this catalog.

## Item and object ESP

Item ESP covers weapons, ammo, consumables, armor, backpacks, valuables, keys, resources, and explosives. Object ESP covers containers, lock and alarm boxes, weapon loot, medical loot, food, resources, valuables, ammo crates, explosive barrels, auto turrets, and corpse loot.

That is the merchant-style list. We do not add POIs that are not in the listing. If checkout renamed a container type after a content drop, trust checkout.

Filter hard. Full item ESP in a loot-heavy settlement is how overlays become unreadable. Keep keys and valuables on. Drop food and junk ammo if the screen fills up.

## 2D radar

The radar lists smooth rotation, labels, player, NPC, creature, item, and object indicators, plus adjustable range and size. Put it where your vanilla minimap already lives. A huge radar in the center of the screen is a report magnet.

Range should match how you play. Short for settlements. Longer for open terrain. Recheck after patches; a client update can change how rotation feels.

## Colors, panic key, and capture

Configurable colors belong in misc, but they save ESP. Dim boxes at night. Keep enemy contrast without neon. Panic key exists for a reason. Test OBS Game Capture locally. Forum threads linked from Complete cover capture leaks better than a shop FAQ.

For a first layout, read the [config presets](/blog/once-human-hacks-config-presets/) post, then save a settlement profile and an open-world profile before you raise every distance slider.
