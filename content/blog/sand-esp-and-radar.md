ESP and radar on Sand Hacks are information layers. They do not pull your crosshair. In SAND: Raiders of Sophie, that information is the difference between walking into an extract camp and swinging wide around a Trampler shadow.

This guide follows merchant module groups for the visual half of [Complete Sand Hacks](/products/sand-hacks/complete/).

## Player and NPC ESP

Core player ESP includes identification, team tags, nicknames, account IDs, distance, health, shield, held item, and kit intel. Add **2D boxes**, **corner boxes**, **skeleton ESP**, **head indicators**, **direction indicators**, and **snaplines** as needed.

**City preset:** corner boxes, distance on, nicknames off, snaplines off, skeleton off if FPS dips.  
**Dune preset:** boxes plus distance, higher max range, direction indicators on for ridge fights.

**NPC ESP** helps with Upior patrols and ambient threats. Filter mentally: not every NPC dot needs a snapline.

## Trampler group

Trampler modules are why Sophie is not just another extraction map:

- **Player Trampler tracking**
- **NPC Trampler tracking**
- **Trampler loot visualization**
- **Trampler radar**

Turn Trampler radar on when you route toward a hijack or when you hear lumbering footsteps but cannot place the deck. Turn loot visualization on when you are farming cracked containers after a fight—not when you are trying to hide in a alley.

Deck fights pair well with [extract and Trampler routing](/blog/sand-extracts-and-tramplers/).

## Item ESP

Merchant item ESP covers weapons, ammunition, consumables, armor, backpacks, valuables, keys, resources, and explosives. Practical filters:

- **Keys** during timed contract runs
- **Aurogen** when object ESP marks crystals nearby
- **Explosives** when you hear barrel chains in industrial blocks

Do not leave every item class on; the screen becomes unreadable before you reach captain quarters.

## 2D radar

Radar modules include smooth rotation, labels, player/NPC/Trampler/item/object indicators, adjustable range, and customizable size. Radar is your compass when verticality and Trampler silhouettes clutter the skyline.

Tips:

- Shrink radar size in cities; expand slightly on dunes
- Lower range downtown to avoid false urgency three blocks out
- Pair with **smoothed compass heading** from misc on Complete licenses

## Object ESP

Object ESP is loot grammar: containers, lock and alarm boxes, typed loot piles, explosive barrels, naval mines, Aurogen crystals, ladders, buried treasure, auto turrets, Dreadnaught markers, Trampler turrets, captain quarters, corpse loot, and projectile shields.

Toggle aggressively:

- **Captain quarters** and **Dreadnaught** when running naval arcs
- **Containers** during generic farm routes
- **Mines** when cutting through beach zones

Confirm the live checkout list—object names track merchant wording.

## Readability rules that save raids

If your HUD looks like a fireworks show, you will hesitate—and hesitation kills extracts. Adopt three rules:

1. **One bright color** for players you might fight.
2. **One muted color** for loot you care about this run.
3. **Everything else off** until the contract changes.

## Vertical fights

Sophie rooftops punish snaplines that draw through three floors. Disable snaplines in city configs; keep head indicators only when you hear footsteps above.

## Audio vs ESP

ESP does not mute the game. Trampler audio still beats radar when fog or dust kicks in. Treat overlays as confirmation, not a substitute for directional sound.

## Patch checklist for ESP users

After updates: reset max distances, re-test Trampler radar near a known route, and record one minute of OBS capture even if you do not stream. Merchant updates sometimes rename object ESP categories—re-read checkout if icons disappear.
